PRIOR

Perceptive Learning for Humanoid Locomotion with Reference Gait Priors

Video 中文

Abstract

Training perceptive humanoid locomotion policies that traverse complex terrains with natural gaits remains challenging, typically requiring multi-stage pipelines, adversarial objectives, or extensive real-world calibration. We present PRIOR, an efficient Isaac Lab framework achieving robust terrain traversal with human-like gaits through three components: (i) a parametric gait generator supplying stable reference trajectories from motion capture without adversarial training, (ii) a GRU-based state estimator inferring terrain geometry from egocentric depth images via self-supervised heightmap reconstruction, and (iii) terrain-adaptive footstep rewards guiding foot placement toward traversable regions. We analyze depth image resolution trade-offs to maximize terrain fidelity under real-time constraints, reducing perceptual overhead without degrading performance. Experiments on six terrain types demonstrate strong generalization, with the full framework achieving 83.5\% success rate and gait quality evaluations confirming improved motion naturalness from the reference gait prior. The framework will be released as open source.
